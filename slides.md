## My main idea

- In economic terms, as firms grow larger, worker quality of life / job
  satisfaction often becomes an externality, sort of like pollution.

---

## OnShift

- Prototype in 2006: find somebody to work a shift by blasting texts to
  qualified and available employees

- By 2009, the software could plan shift schedules for (theoretically)
  any workforce.

- Employees could trade shifts with each other, apply for open shifts,
  modify schedule, etc.

- Employee buy-in was critical at the start

---

## OnShift later features

- Small customers don't pay much and we had investors pushing for growth

- Big nursing home chains wanted features like:

  - cut scheduled hours to regulatory minimums
  - recommend lowest-paid employees for any open shift

---

## Is kinda like Enshittification

- Once you're selling a product to the boss who is going to use it to
  spy on / control their workers, the product doesn't need to be fun to
  use

- User buy-in isn't a bottleneck like it once was

- the people using the product weren't happy to see me anymore

## SpotHero

- Marketplace app for parking. Launched in Chicago. Allows you to
  reserve a parking spot in the future

- At first, we needed the attendants to promote the app

- A few years after launch, nobody cares if the attendants liked it,
  because the garage owners mandated it

## SofaConcerts (1)

- web marketplace that matches musicians and venues

- Average venue gets 3 artists for every open spot

- Artists get more gigs (like by adding house party shows during tours)

- Artists get guaranteed payment because funds go in escrow before
  performance

---

## SofaConcerts (2)

- They have turned down several investment / acquisition offers

> "we make it easier for touring artists to visit smaller villages and
> towns."

- Without investors that pushing for aggressive growth, they can focus
  on their social goals

## How does AI fit in with this?

- coding agents really are very powerful

> "It's like programming wearing power armor"

- I pay $20/month for a coding agent that replaces what two other people
  would do full-time (or I'd have to do it)

---

## The SaaS business model

0. Raise (or have) a few million bucks based on an idea

1. Hire programmers to build a software application customers can't live
   without

2. Sell subscriptions

3. Add features to keep customers hooked

Investors fund development because they are betting you can execute on
your vision.

---

## "I oneshotted photoshop"

- ArtCraft is using coding agents to build an open-source implementation
  of everything that Adobe offers.

- People seem to like it

- This is just one example of how code may not be as valuable as it used
  to be

---

## Causes for optimism

- If your boss and you have a good rapport, maybe the era of coding
  agents means it is cheaper now to make tools you like to use

- coding agents mean small teams can build out bigger projects now
  without taking investment money, so they can stay true to their vision

- There are a lot of really good open-source coding agents.  People are
  working on running these on low-cost hardware (in other words, not
  hyperscale data centers).
