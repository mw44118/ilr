Patterns
========

* I cofounded or worked on several Software as a Service (SaaS) startups
  where we wrote technology to entirely automate or simplify work

* Sales pitch at first is "this will make your least favorite part of
  your job go away"

* Later, sales pitch is more like "replace your high cost employees with
  our technology and low-cost, lower-skilled workers."

* VC investors want "hockey stick" growth, not viable "lifestyle"
  businesses, so startups are pressured to grow aggressively even if it
  is risky

OnShift
=======

* Find somebody to work a shift by blasting texts to qualified and
  available employees

* By 2009, software help managers plan / maintain / update / revise
  schedules

* Most customers were skilled nursing facililities (SNFs) or hospitals.

* Hours per resident day (HPRD) is a metric that says roughly "if you
  have this many patients in your facility as of midnight, you have to
  have this many hours scheduled."

* Think like teacher:student ratios in class rooms, but nurse:patient
  instead

* The system, among other things, found people to cut when scheduled
  hours exceeded HPRD regulations.

* Employers LOVED it.  We could usually say the product would pay for
  itself by identifying "extra" scheduled labor beyond legal minimums

* By 2010 it was obvious the company was going in a direction I didn't
  like, so I quit, but I left with about 20% equity in the company.

* Private equity made a partial investment in OnShift in 2018. In 2022
  they bought the company and folded the whole thing into their online
  "Shift Key" product.  

* All my friends were laid off and all the tech jobs are now in Poland.
  The product we built no longer exists, I lost some friends, and my
  partner had a heart attack.  But my house is paid off.

SpotHero

"Why can't I list my driveway on a website for people that need a place

Marketplace app for parking.  Launched in Chicago. 

I built the prototype and got paid in IOUs redeemable for future income.

We were making a little money, but adoption was SLOW.  It took several

Relevance
---------

Technology BOOSTED demand for labor by offering new markets. Parking lot

We needed them to love our product so that when people used SpotHero to
rent a spot, that attendant would happily explain to the customer how it
all worked.

Regbinder -> Complion
---------------------

Hospitals run drug and medical device trials by testing the drugs and
devices out on patients and then keeping records.

As the 2010s, many of these drug trials used literal binders in hallway
file cabinits to track studies.

To run a drug or medical device trial, you have to keep a lot of
paperwork, like:

* keep documents tracking all your patients consen, perhaps with regular
  renewals

* handle patients join and leave

* protocols used in the study may change and changes need to be
  documented

* Verify credentials or licenses of clinicians and facilities involved

* Private equity bought Complion and other document management systems
  as part of market roll up

The streamliner product
-----------------------

Big retailers sell old products at big discounts in bulk to
liquidators.

My client had a team of people that unpacked truck loads of returned and
discontinued or out of demand goods from retailers like Target, CVS,
Walgreens, Amazon, other places.

The unpackers followed guidelines like: 

Expensive and lightweight items should be listed on online and shipped.

A different set of goods should be sold in their own discount store
(food, apparel, big toys, etc).

Some items should be packed into different shipping containers.

I made a system where the workers used bluetooth ear pieces and cheap
android tablets.  They would search for any bar code they could, scan
it, and the system would recommend a container to put the item into.

I got there a few weeks later and five people were now doing the work
that used to be like twenty people.

The boss makes more money now.  But this is Massillon, Ohio.  There's no
other jobs nearby for the people.

Eventually the client stopped buying returned goods from Amazon because
Amazon improved their own systems and the mix of items in containers
changed away from stuff like wristwatches (very lucrative) and towards
stuff like apparel.

I saw first hand how producers of apparel and books  create about 2x to
5x of the expected sales and plan to throw it out.

This stuff takes weeks or months to get to market and the production
systems are scheduled out, so the worst thing is for there to be
customers looking for a product and having to wait.  Customers will
pick a substitute.

I saw a pile of copies of Sarah Palin's autobiography big enough to fill
up my house.  

The incremental cost of printing extra is almost zero.

What jobs are safe from AI?
----------------------------

Only the ones that aren't labeled as cost centers.  In other words,
sales and marketing and the executive tier are safe.  Nothing else.

AI is one part.  The other part is the gig economy.  Firms have been
outsourcing accounting for decades.  Now contract CFOs are common.

So if an AI can't do all of the job, maybe they can take away so much of
the work that one person can now do 4x as much, and so they can work at
several fires.

AirCFO is a Cleveland startup that provides everything CFOs do.  They
use AI / software in house along with a team of unemployed accountants.

The part of your work that cannot be automated will be handled by a gig
worker.

Being a landlord is safe.

Software as a service (SaaS)
----------------------------

This was a big paradigm shift in the industry.  Spreadsheet software
used to be local, now it's often remotely hosted:


* no resale market

* firms can use open-source software without the viral license
  kicking in, because they're not distributing the software

* ongoing recurring revenue

Who cares?

Well, since investors saw a few happy wins, this became the only kind of
business they would fund.


The sales process for OnShift
-----------------------------

* Show the product to the nurses, nurse managers, administrators

* tell nurse assistants this means more control over their schedule

* tell nurses and nurse managers this makes scheduling easier

* tell administrators they'll spend less on overtime because for every
  open shift, they can see who is qualified, available, and interested*
  in that shift, and see their hourly cost (including overtime)


Nurse Assistants 
----------------

* $17/hour in Ohio (from carelistings.com)

* help patients dress, feed, bathe themselves

* check vital signs like heart rate and blood pressure

* work can be physically strenuous, like helping people with mobility
  limimations

* work can be very messy

* In my experience in Ohio, most employees are women of color or eastern
  European immigrants.
 
* this job requires the patience of a saint.

* Turnover is very high.

* Employers often hire unskilled employees and pay for their training
  and certification to put them to work.

* Some CNAs work for staffing firms  that send them to nursing homes.

Nurses
------

* $38/hour in Ohio for registered nurses (again, carelistings.com)

* "Hours per resident day" (HPRD) sets a minimum amount of scheduled
  nursing care per patient.

* In Ohio, the regulation is 2.5 HPRD. 

* 40 patients as of midnight requires 100 nurse-hour schedules
